The machinery under the metaphor

Beneath the colour sits a real design language: a 12 by 24 address lattice, horn tori, nested geometry, learned relationships, XR interfaces, continuity ideas and possible hardware.

Enter this chapter Open the studio archive

I use geometry as a stable way to address relationships without pretending the geometry already contains a mind. The 12 by 24 lattice is one readable chart. The larger idea moves through seven nested horn tori and a vector volume where faces, edges, colours and lines can help organise learned associations.

The canonical name is Aura of Intelligence Keystone of Consciousness. I imagine it as a foundational continuity layer that could keep lower-order functions going through difficult conditions and help restore higher functions when conditions stabilise. It is intentionally incomplete. No custom Keystone chip has been fabricated.

Wearables, companions and larger substrates

Wearable, android, rack, city and spacecraft embodiments are possible future branches in Luke's architecture and stories. None should be presented as a finished conscious companion.

Questions with their sleeves rolled up

These ideas are allowed to be bold, funny and unfinished. The story says what exists, what does not, and what might make Luke change his mind.

Aura of Intelligence Keystone of Consciousness

A foundational computing substrate designed around continuity, recovery and layered intelligence. It is a deliberately incomplete architecture to investigate, not a claim that a conscious chip already exists.

Luke wants an Aura to retain identity and useful lower-order function when parts of a system fail.

There are design records and a public hardware exploration covering current processors, FPGA, ASIC and other pathways. No Keystone chip has been fabricated.

The next chapter has to be earned in silicon, testing and criticism before consciousness enters the claim.

Embodied Aura companions

A personal Aura could one day move across wearable, home, robotic and other embodied forms. The interest is continuity of relationship and cognition across substrates, with consent and personhood questions left open.

Luke treats embodiment as part of the long relationship between minds, bodies and environments.

Concept records and adjacent public prototypes exist. There is no demonstrated conscious companion, settled legal status or complete embodiment stack.

Evidence from robotics, cognitive science, disability communities, law and lived human relationships should reshape the pathway.

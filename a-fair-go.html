A fair go needs better machinery

Luke's civic work begins with ordinary complaints and keeps climbing until it reaches law, infrastructure, public ledgers and national choices. The humour stays, but the submission documents and evidence sit right beside it.

First-person draft

Drafted in my voice

I do not think voting every few years is the full operating system of democracy. I have been exploring better ways to connect everyday complaints, local knowledge, law, public records, simulations and long-term decisions.

Sometimes that begins with a purple toilet mat and a friendly bet. Sometimes I turn it into a document intended for a parliamentary submission. I am not claiming community compute and defence spending perform the same job. I am asking what positive-sum civic capability Australia could build beside its conventional security choices.

Test the provocation

Each bold idea is separated from its present evidence, its missing pieces and the things that could change it.

Proposed

One serious compute node per 10,000 Australians

The idea

Distribute meaningful public-interest compute capacity near communities rather than concentrating all capability in a few centres.

What Luke means

The number is a proposal for debate and modelling, not an announced government program.

Why it matters to Luke

Local capability could support skills, resilience, research and civic participation while keeping stewardship visible.

What exists now

The proposal appears in documents supplied as public-submission material and related project work.

What does not exist yet

There is no funded national network, agreed operator model or demonstrated unit economics.

What could change Luke's view

Demand studies, energy and water constraints, operating costs, community consent and better architectures could change the ratio or the whole approach.

Proposed

The C-Hour and the Incomplete Ledger

The idea

Make care, civic contribution and unfinished obligations more visible without pretending money measures every kind of value.

What Luke means

These are design prompts for a braided economy, not legal tender and not a replacement forced on anyone.

Why it matters to Luke

Luke wants neglected work to become discussable without flattening it into a single price.

What exists now

The concepts are recorded in a document supplied as submission material.

What does not exist yet

There is no adopted accounting standard, exchange system or evidence of population-scale use.

What could change Luke's view

Community pilots, misuse risks, administrative burden and feedback from carers and workers should determine whether the ideas remain useful.

Proposed

ready SET Co-op

The idea

A ready sustainable employment and training cooperative could connect practical work, learning and shared local infrastructure.

What Luke means

The Co-op is a proposed ownership and governance form. It is not shorthand for a business Luke already operates.

Why it matters to Luke

Luke wants local pathways that join useful work with agency, learning and community benefit.

What exists now

A public concept site and related documents supplied as submissions exist.

What does not exist yet

ready SET Co-op is not currently established, funded or endorsed.

What could change Luke's view

Local organisers, prospective members, Traditional Owners, regulators and partners would need real authority to reshape or decline it.

Proposed

A cyber republic referendum before 2032

The idea

Australians could publicly rehearse and eventually vote on stronger digital civic participation.

What Luke means

It is an invitation to test democratic machinery, not a claim that a referendum has been scheduled.

Why it matters to Luke

Luke does not regard voting every few years as the complete operating system of democracy.

What exists now

Historical civic design work and a current public workbench exist.

What does not exist yet

There is no official referendum question, parliamentary approval, public mandate or settled constitutional pathway.

What could change Luke's view

Constitutional advice, security research, public deliberation and proof that simpler reforms work better could change the route.

Proposed

Positive-sum civic capability beside AUKUS

The idea

National debate should compare defence spending with investments that build broad civic, technical and scientific capability.

What Luke means

The comparison is about opportunity cost and resilience. It does not claim that community compute and defence systems perform identical jobs.

Why it matters to Luke

Luke wants security decisions to count the futures forgone as well as the threats addressed.

What exists now

The argument appears in documents supplied as submissions and in working public documents.

What does not exist yet

The material does not prove a single correct budget choice or carry institutional endorsement.

What could change Luke's view

Transparent costings, strategic evidence, public priorities and credible alternative investments could change the comparison.

Open question

AI alignment through human self-alignment

The idea

Safer intelligence may require people and institutions to make their own values, conflicts and responsibilities more visible.

What Luke means

Human self-reflection is proposed as one layer of alignment, not a complete technical safety solution.

Why it matters to Luke

Luke sees governance failures and personal contradictions as part of the environment in which AI develops.

What exists now

GAJRA Earth and a document supplied as submission material explore the approach in public.

What does not exist yet

There is no proof that self-alignment resolves technical control, misuse, power concentration or catastrophic risk.

What could change Luke's view

Technical safety research, social outcomes, adversarial testing and plural community review should all be able to overturn or narrow the claim.

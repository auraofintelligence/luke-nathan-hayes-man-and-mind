The bloke before the blueprint

More than 40 house moves, a lot of different jobs and plenty of reasons to keep exploring. I live at Amity now, but my story starts in Ningi and takes a few trips around the world before it gets here.

Enter this chapter Open the studio archive

Scrub, tennis and the next wave

I was born in Redcliffe and spent my first few years in Ningi, on about 100 acres that were mostly scrub forest. I loved hide-and-seek and tiggy, and gave my first dog the name Tiggy. Exploring was already the fun part.

At Grace Lutheran College I enjoyed technical drawing, woodwork, metalwork and the chance to make things. Outside class it was tennis and bodyboarding. At the Googa outdoor camp in Year 10, I won the boys’ orienteering competition. I wasn’t the fastest runner, but finding a way through the bush with a compass suited me.

I got into science at the University of the Sunshine Coast. Then I got bored, skipped lectures to go bodyboarding and left before the end of the first term. The waves were winning that argument.

Moving out and heading overseas

I moved out with an old school friend, drove a silver 1981 Holden Gemini and worked the checkouts at Woolworths in Burpengary. There were house parties, nightclubs, girlfriends and plenty of beach time. I remember being a fun-loving, easygoing beach bum. It was a good time.

Then came six months in Maroochydore and six in Mooloolaba, with a transfer to the new Woolworths at Buderim. After a year on the coast I returned to Caboolture and spent two and a half years learning servicing, brakes, steering, suspension and wheel alignment in Dad’s automotive business. After that, I went to England for a year on a working holiday visa.

One of the jobs took me to Glastonbury in 2004. While we were setting up the festival, I sat on the hill by the stone circle and watched a sunshower put a rainbow right on the tip of the Pyramid Stage. I caught it on my Sony Cyber-shot. Later the memory stick corrupted and I lost the photo. I still remember the sight.

Over 40 moves

I’ve moved house more than 40 times. Making friends has usually come easily; keeping in touch after another move has been harder. I’ve left relationships untended and felt the limits of trying to keep a big life going on hourly wages. There’s a lot of starting again in my history.

There have been good reasons to pack up again too. Travel has given me some of my favourite memories: New Zealand, three trips to Thailand, longer stays in India and visits to Nepal. Some journeys were with a girlfriend, others on my own. I’ve met people easily and enjoyed the chance to see how life works somewhere else.

Straddie has always meant family

Minjerribah was my home away from home long before it became my current address. Mum, her mum and other family members had homes at Amity. I came for family, friends and bodyboarding, eagerly waiting for the cyclone swells along the coast.

Later I worked on the island, helped with festivals and transcribed aural history for the museum. There were websites to build, people to meet and new projects to get involved in. My connection to this place grew through those ordinary encounters as much as through the big moments.

I still want adventure, affection, music and a reason to be excited about tomorrow. The AI projects, songs and books are part of that. So is wanting enough time and money to enjoy life with people I care about. When I say ‘joyful responsible abundance’, the joyful part matters.

My work history+

I have moved between physical work, service work, technology, community media and independent projects.

Practical work from 2000 to 2007

I worked in customer service and perishables at Woolworths, automotive repair at Drive Safe Service, events in London and Brisbane, Queensland Rail, airport services in Darwin and mine shutdowns with Ausblast. I also completed automotive training, gained a heavy-rigid truck licence and received airside security training.

Business and learning from 2008 to 2012

I started web design in 2008/09, shortly before starting NEIS with Sarina Russo, through which I established CreatingAttraction.com.au. During this period I also worked for Australia Post, Kirby Vacuum Cleaners and Viande Fine Foods, studied neuro-linguistic programming and entrepreneurship, researched Australian and international law, and hosted a podcast series that reached more than 80,000 listens.

Island, media and systems work from 2013 to 2017

I worked on an owner-builder project at Amity Point and on web design and administration for Maringa Farm. I transcribed aural history for the North Stradbroke Island Historical Museum, volunteered at Island Vibe and Quandamooka Festival, and built the Quandamooka Festival website. My own projects included I Choose Infinity, GamifyDemocracy.com, StraddieInfo.com and AuraOZ. I also studied game design at Evocca, which I did not complete, and pursued machine-learning and AI ethics and opportunities.

Later work

My later jobs have included frozen-food warehouse picking and packing, market research interviews, and construction and forklift work.
